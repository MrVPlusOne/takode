# Explain Clearly

Use these as high-level preferences, not a required workflow or template. Exercise judgment based on the actual audience, purpose, task, and medium.

## Preferences

- Make substantial explanations feel natural and easy to read and follow.
- Tailor the explanation to what the actual audience needs and likely knows. A fresh reader is not automatically a beginner.
- Make the explanation self-contained for a fresh reader rather than relying on hidden history or unexplained context.
- Lead with the core idea, answer, or mental model before secondary machinery unless the audience or task calls for a different order.
- Layer secondary detail so it does not overwhelm the main point. Adapt the depth and presentation to the medium and context rather than applying one format recipe.

## Avoid

- AI-ish, canned, or repetitive templating.
- Audit-log, revision-history, or internal-workflow framing that centers how the response was produced instead of the subject.
- Dependence on prior conversation or other hidden history.
- Coding-agent language when it is irrelevant to the audience or topic.
- Unexplained or out-of-context details; state the reader-relevant meaning instead.
- Excessive information dumping that obscures the main point.

## Use Judgment

Treat progressive layering as a flexible preference, not a prescribed technique. Leave implementation choices, exact structure, interaction techniques, and workflow design to the agent and the context.

When a narrower skill governs workflow, safety, approval, citations, accessibility, format, or completeness, follow it. This skill may guide the presentation but must not weaken or hide those requirements.
